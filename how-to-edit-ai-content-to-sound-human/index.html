How to edit AI content to sound human — without sounding like AI

AI can give you a blog post, email or social media update in seconds. But if you publish it exactly as it comes out, it can sound bland, repetitive and strangely unlike you.

The answer isn’t to hide the fact that you used AI. It’s to edit the content so it reflects your own experience, opinions and way of speaking.

The short answer

Don’t try to make AI content “beat” an AI detector. Make it sound like you.

Add your own experience, remove generic phrases, check the facts and rewrite anything you wouldn’t actually say to a customer.

Why does AI content so often sound like AI?

Have you ever read something online and thought:

“That sounds like it was written by ChatGPT.”

You probably weren’t imagining it.

AI-generated writing often has a familiar style.

It’s usually very tidy. Every section has a heading. Every point gets neatly explained. The paragraphs can be similar in length and the language can sound polished without actually saying very much.

For example:

“In today’s fast-paced business environment, small businesses can leverage AI to unlock new opportunities, improve productivity and enhance customer experiences.”

There’s nothing technically wrong with that sentence.

But would you actually say it to a customer?

Probably not.

You might say:

“AI can save you time, but only if you use it for the right jobs.”

That’s shorter.

It’s clearer.

And it sounds like something a real business owner might actually say.

That’s the difference.

The problem isn’t AI. It’s generic writing.

There’s a temptation to ask:

“How do I make this sound less like AI?”

That’s not quite the right question.

Instead, ask:

“Would I actually say this?”

That’s a much better test.

AI doesn’t know your business like you do.

It doesn’t know the customer who always comes into your shop on a Friday afternoon.

It doesn’t know which boiler problem you see every winter.

It doesn’t know which haircut customers ask for most often.

It doesn’t know why you stopped selling a particular product.

And it doesn’t know the question customers ask you every week.

You do.

That’s where the human part comes from.

The easiest way to make AI content sound human is to put more of yourself into it.

Use AI for the first draft – not the final word

AI can be very useful for getting started.

You could ask it to:

  • Create a blog post outline.
  • Turn your notes into a first draft.
  • Suggest questions customers might ask.
  • Rewrite something that’s difficult to understand.
  • Shorten a long email.
  • Suggest social media ideas.
  • Give you several headline options.
  • Turn a long document into a simple summary.

That’s where AI can save you time.

But don’t confuse a first draft with finished content.

Think about it like this.

If you asked a member of staff to write something for your website, would you publish their first draft without reading it?

Probably not.

You’d check it.

You’d change things.

You’d add your own knowledge.

You might cut half of it.

Do the same with AI.

Delete the phrases you’d never say

One of the quickest ways to make AI writing sound more natural is to remove the phrases that appear again and again in AI-generated content.

Look out for phrases such as:

  • “In today’s fast-paced world”
  • “It’s important to note that”
  • “Whether you’re a small business or a large organisation”
  • “There are several key benefits”
  • “Let’s delve into”
  • “By leveraging”
  • “In conclusion”
  • “A game-changer”
  • “Unlock the potential”
  • “Take your business to the next level”
  • “Navigate the challenges”
  • “A seamless experience”

The problem isn’t that these phrases are always wrong.

The problem is that they’re everywhere.

They often make a simple point sound much more complicated than it needs to be.

Instead of:

“There are several key benefits to leveraging AI within your business.”

Try:

“AI can save you time. But it won’t save you time on every job.”

Much better.

Tip

Keep your own banned-words list

You already know which words don’t sound like you.

Write them down.

For example:

Words and phrases I don’t want AI to use:

  • Leverage
  • Utilise
  • Game-changer
  • Cutting-edge
  • Unlock
  • Delve
  • In today’s fast-paced world

Add your list to your AI prompt.

You can also keep it beside you when you’re editing.

Over time, you’ll spot these phrases almost immediately.

Add details only you would know

This is probably the most effective way to make AI content feel human.

AI can write:

“Our café takes pride in serving quality coffee to our customers.”

So can every other café.

There’s nothing wrong with it.

There’s just nothing particularly interesting about it.

Now compare it with:

“We open at 7am because a surprising number of our regulars are builders and tradespeople who want a coffee before heading to their first job.”

That’s different.

It’s specific.

It’s believable.

And it tells the reader something about the business.

The same idea works in almost every trade.

A hairdresser might write:

“Most colour appointments take around three hours. If you’re coming straight from work, bring something to eat.”

A plumber might write:

“If your boiler pressure keeps dropping, don’t keep topping it up every few days and hope it’ll sort itself out. Something is causing the pressure to fall.”

A butcher might write:

“Friday afternoon is when we get most of the questions about what to cook on Sunday.”

A retailer might say:

“If you’re buying this as a present, keep the receipt. We’ve had quite a few customers come back because the recipient already owns one.”

Those details make content feel human.

Why?

Because they’re based on real experience.

Add your opinion

AI tends to be careful.

Very careful.

It often tries to avoid taking a strong position.

That can make the writing boring.

You probably have opinions about your business.

You know which products are worth buying.

You know which aren’t.

You know which mistakes customers make.

You know which jobs take longer than people expect.

You know what you’d recommend to a customer standing in front of you.

Say it.

Instead of:

“There are several factors to consider when choosing a coffee machine.”

Try:

“If you’re only making 20 coffees a day, I wouldn’t spend thousands on a commercial machine.”

That’s advice.

And advice is more useful than a list of generic statements.

Your experience is one of the things that makes your content different from thousands of other websites.

Don’t be afraid to delete half of it

AI has no problem writing 2,000 words.

You might only need 800.

That’s one of the biggest traps when using AI for content.

You ask for a comprehensive article.

AI gives you one.

Then you publish all of it because it’s already written.

But your customer might not want 2,000 words.

They might want an answer.

Ask yourself:

“Does this sentence help the reader?”

If it doesn’t, cut it.

If you’ve already made the point somewhere else, cut it.

If a paragraph simply repeats the heading, cut it.

If you’re explaining something your customer already understands, shorten it.

Good editing isn’t about adding more.

Quite often, it’s about taking things away.

Vary your sentences

AI often produces a very regular rhythm.

Short sentence.

Medium sentence.

Long explanation.

Then another short sentence.

Real people don’t always write like that.

Mix things up.

For example:

“Don’t do this.

If you’re using AI to write customer emails, read the message before you send it. It sounds obvious, but it’s surprisingly easy to click send because the draft looks polished.

That’s when mistakes happen.”

It feels more natural.

You don’t need to deliberately make every sentence different.

Just avoid making every paragraph sound as though it came from the same template.

Read it out loud

This is one of the simplest editing tricks there is.

Read your article aloud.

Actually say the words.

You’ll quickly notice sentences that don’t sound right.

You’ll hear repeated words.

You’ll find paragraphs that are too long.

You’ll spot phrases you wouldn’t normally use.

And you’ll notice where the writing starts to sound like a brochure.

If you stumble over a sentence, your reader might stumble over it too.

You don’t need special software.

Your own voice is the test.

Write the way you speak

This doesn’t mean writing down every word exactly as you’d say it.

Nobody wants to read:

“Well, basically, you know, what I’m trying to say is…”

Instead, imagine you’re explaining the subject to one customer who has asked you a question.

What would you say?

Write that down.

Then tidy it up.

For example, imagine a customer asks:

“Is AI safe to use for my business?”

You probably wouldn’t answer:

“There are a number of factors that businesses should take into consideration when evaluating the potential risks associated with artificial intelligence.”

You might say:

“It can be, but don’t put private customer information into an AI tool until you know what happens to that information.”

That’s how you should aim to write.

Use real questions from your customers

This is another easy way to make AI content more useful.

Think about the questions you answer again and again.

A plumber might hear:

“Can I still use the boiler if the pressure keeps dropping?”

A hairdresser:

“Can I wash my hair before I have it coloured?”

A café owner:

“Do you have anything dairy-free?”

A retailer:

“Can I return this if I’ve opened the packaging?”

These are much better starting points than:

“Write an article about plumbing.”

Give AI the real question.

Then answer it using your own experience.

Now you’ve got content that has a reason to exist.

Don’t let AI invent your experience

This is one of the most important rules.

AI can easily write:

“We’ve helped hundreds of customers solve this problem.”

But have you?

It might write:

“Our customers tell us this is one of their favourite products.”

Do they?

It could write:

“We’ve been serving the local community for more than 20 years.”

Have you?

Never let AI invent:

  • Customer testimonials.
  • Reviews.
  • Business history.
  • Qualifications.
  • Awards.
  • Statistics.
  • Results.
  • Customer numbers.
  • Case studies.
  • Personal experiences.

If it’s about your business, check it.

If you don’t know whether it’s true, remove it.

Caution

AI can make your business sound more experienced than it really is

This happens surprisingly often.

Ask AI to write a website page for a new business and it may automatically produce phrases such as:

  • “With years of experience…”
  • “Trusted by customers across the UK…”
  • “Our team of experts…”
  • “Proven results…”
  • “A reputation built on excellence…”

They sound good.

But are they true?

If not, don’t use them.

Your business doesn’t need to sound bigger than it is.

Being a small business isn’t something you need to hide.

In fact, your size can be one of your strengths.

Try giving AI your own rough draft

Here’s an approach that can work particularly well.

Write the rough version yourself.

Don’t worry about grammar.

Don’t worry about making it perfect.

Just get your thoughts down.

Then ask AI to help edit it.

For example:

“Here are my notes. Improve the structure, spelling and grammar, but keep my opinions, examples and tone. Don’t add facts or claims that aren’t in my notes. Keep the language simple and natural.”

That’s very different from:

“Write me a 1,500-word article about my business.”

In the first example, you’re giving AI something real to work with.

You’re still providing the experience and knowledge.

AI is helping with the editing.

Use AI as an editor – not as your identity

This is probably the simplest way to think about it.

AI can help you:

  • Structure your thoughts.
  • Fix spelling mistakes.
  • Suggest a headline.
  • Shorten a paragraph.
  • Explain something more clearly.
  • Find repetition.
  • Turn notes into a draft.
  • Give you alternative ways to say something.

But you should provide:

  • Your experience.
  • Your opinions.
  • Your examples.
  • Your stories.
  • Your judgement.
  • Your personality.
  • Your final approval.

That’s what makes the content yours.

Don’t try to fool AI detectors

You may have seen websites that claim they can tell whether something was written by AI.

Be careful with them.

AI detection isn’t a magic test.

A detector can flag human writing as AI-generated. It can also fail to identify AI-generated writing.

So don’t spend your time changing perfectly good writing simply because you’re worried about what a detector might say.

Ask a better question:

“Is this useful, accurate and genuinely representative of my business?”

That’s what matters.

If you’ve used AI to help edit something you’ve written yourself, there’s no need to turn the whole process into a game of trying to fool detection software.

Should you tell people you used AI?

There’s no single answer for every piece of content.

If AI corrected a few spelling mistakes, you probably don’t need to announce it.

If AI helped turn your notes into a blog post, the situation may be different depending on what the content is and how much AI contributed.

And if AI is talking directly to your customers, making decisions about them or providing a significant part of a service, transparency becomes much more important.

Don’t pretend an AI system is a person when it isn’t.

And never use AI to create fake reviews or pretend customers had experiences they didn’t have.

Your reputation is worth much more than a few minutes saved.

The aim isn’t to hide AI

There’s nothing wrong with using AI to help you create content.

If you’re running a butcher’s shop, café, plumbing business or independent retailer, you’ve got better things to do than spend all afternoon staring at a blank Word document.

AI can help.

The problem is publishing content that’s:

  • Generic.
  • Inaccurate.
  • Repetitive.
  • Full of empty claims.
  • Unrelated to your real experience.
  • Written in a voice that doesn’t belong to your business.

Good AI-assisted content doesn’t need to fool anyone.

It needs to be useful.

It needs to be accurate.

And it needs to sound as though a real person who knows their subject had something to say.

Your five-minute human edit

Before you publish AI-assisted content, ask yourself these five questions.

1. Would I actually say this?

If you wouldn’t, rewrite it.

2. Does this contain something only I could have written?

If not, add a real example, opinion or experience.

3. Has AI invented anything?

Check every number, name, date, statistic and claim.

4. Have I removed the waffle?

Cut anything that doesn’t help the reader.

5. Does it sound like my business?

If someone who knows you read it, would they recognise your voice?

If the answer to all five is yes, you’re probably close to finished.

A simple AI content checklist

Before publishing, run through this list:

  • ☐ I’ve read the entire piece.
  • ☐ It sounds like me.
  • ☐ I’ve removed generic AI phrases.
  • ☐ I’ve added real examples.
  • ☐ I’ve added my own opinions where appropriate.
  • ☐ I’ve checked the facts.
  • ☐ I’ve removed anything AI invented.
  • ☐ I’ve cut unnecessary sections.
  • ☐ I’ve varied the sentence lengths.
  • ☐ I’ve read it aloud.
  • ☐ It answers a real customer question.
  • ☐ I’d be happy putting my name to it.

That’s it.

You don’t need a complicated system.

You just need to take responsibility for what’s published.

AI FAQs

Questions people ask about editing AI content

These are the practical questions UK business owners are asking about making AI-generated content sound human.

How do I make AI writing sound more human?

Read it aloud, remove generic phrases and add your own experience and opinions. The biggest improvement usually comes from adding specific details that AI couldn’t know without you.

Why does ChatGPT writing sound so generic?

AI has learned patterns from huge amounts of written material. When you ask it for a general article, it tends to produce safe and broadly applicable language.

Give it specific information about your business, your customers and how you normally speak. You’ll usually get a much better starting point.

Should I edit AI-generated content before publishing it?

Yes.

Always read through AI-generated content before publishing it. Check facts, statistics, names, dates, claims about your business and anything that could affect a customer’s decision.

AI can produce convincing mistakes.

Can Google tell if content was written by AI?

Don’t build your content strategy around trying to fool AI detection systems.

Focus on producing useful, original content that demonstrates real experience and answers the reader’s question.

That’s a much better long-term approach.

How do I stop ChatGPT using the same phrases all the time?

Tell it which phrases you don’t want it to use.

You can also give it examples of your own writing and explain how you normally speak to customers.

Keeping a short list of words and phrases you don’t use can help too.

Is it OK to use AI to write my business blog?

Yes.

There’s nothing inherently wrong with using AI to help create business content.

The important thing is what you publish.

Check the facts. Add your own experience. Remove generic material. Make sure the final article genuinely helps the reader.

And put your name to it only when you’re happy with it.

⚠️ Evidence note: There’s no reliable test that can guarantee whether a piece of writing was produced by a person or AI.

AI detection tools can produce false positives and false negatives. For a small business, the better test is whether your content is accurate, useful, original and genuinely reflects your business.

The advice in this article is therefore about quality and trust, not about trying to fool AI detection software.

AI can be a useful writing assistant. But you’re still responsible for what you publish.

Make AI sound like you

Use AI to save time, but don’t hand over your voice.

Add your experience, check the facts and edit the final draft until you’d be happy saying the words to a real customer.
